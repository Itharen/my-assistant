A metric told us we were losing 23 voice inputs.

It was true. It was also meaningless.

We had just shipped raw-audio preservation for our voice pipeline: every utterance kept, alongside the transcript. It felt like overhead at the time.

Then the funnel reported 23 utterances lost after recognition, and the obvious move was to go fix that path.

Instead we opened the audio. All 23 were fragments of 0.3 to 2.3 seconds — breathing, sentence tails — into which the recogniser had hallucinated a polite "Thank you." Not one lost sentence. The real defect was somewhere else entirely: a 30-second window silently truncating long recordings.

Without the raw audio, "23 lost inputs" would have been unfalsifiable. We would have hardened the wrong path, watched the number stay flat, and concluded the problem was hard.

A counter tells you how many. It never tells you what.

If you run agent systems in production, keep the artifact, not just the metric. Storage is cheap. Debugging a number you cannot open is not.
